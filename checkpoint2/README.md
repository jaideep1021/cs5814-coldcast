## What ran

The experiment reused the pinned Checkpoint I predictions and item embeddings for 122 eligible system–source pairs across five sources. Random, adaptive uncertainty, and semantic diversity selection were compared on identical pools and recorded outcomes. The study used 12 acquisition trials, three original training seeds, three base predictors, and six budgets (0, 1, 3, 7, 15, and 31). This produced 237,168 pair-level scores. No new training or dataset downloads were needed.

The runner records SHA-256 hashes for all four input artifacts. Acquisition uses a separate function that receives only acquisition logits, the selection order, and a callback for revealed outcomes. The runner verified that acquisition and evaluation groups were disjoint, every order contained distinct groups, and all policies gave identical zero-budget scores for a given pair and starting predictor. Replaying the original method recovered the saved Checkpoint I results within 1e-15 Brier. The updated solver changed the corresponding score by at most 0.000053 on any replayed pair and budget. A repeat run regenerated byte-identical CSVs and figures.

## What to show

1. Show the Checkpoint I zero-label comparison. Constant 0.5 beat every fitted model on the five held-out sources.
2. Open the Checkpoint II learning curves. With random acquisition, metadata logistic regression improves from **0.2474** Brier at zero labels to **0.1943** at three and **0.1633** at 31.
3. Compare the fusion curves at three labels. Uncertainty sampling gets **0.2142** versus **0.2024** for random selection. Over the full curve, its Brier ALC is **0.20617** versus **0.20074** for random.
4. Show the source differences. Metadata diversity improves pair-weighted ALC by **0.00030**, but worsens equal-source ALC by **0.00249**. The gain depends on how benchmarks are weighted.
5. Explain the conclusion. Limited-label adaptation helps. This experiment does not establish a consistently better selection policy than repeated random acquisition.

## Reproduce or demo

From the repository root, with the existing Checkpoint I environment and local artifacts:

```bash
checkpoint1/.venv/bin/python checkpoint2/src/run_acquisition.py
MPLCONFIGDIR="$PWD/checkpoint2/tmp/matplotlib" checkpoint1/.venv/bin/python checkpoint2/src/plot_results.py
```

The experiment requires the Checkpoint I saved `test_predictions.parquet`, `adaptation_pools.json`, `adaptation_by_pair.csv`, and `real_embeddings.npz`. If these are missing, regenerate them using the [Checkpoint I reproduction steps](../checkpoint1/REAL_RESULTS.md#reproduce). The public results contain aggregate scores and figures. Raw data and response-level predictions remain local.
